Wise Company's emergency food supply line now sells under the ReadyWise brand, following a 2019 rebrand. The core products stayed intact: freeze-dried and dehydrated meals in foil pouches, packed into resealable buckets, rated for up to 25 years on the shelf. The kits range from a 72-hour starter to a full one-year cache. The main buying question is whether the calorie count, sodium level, and serving sizes match what your household actually needs, not whether the bucket looks good on a shelf.

That gap between the label claim and your real daily requirement is where most purchases go wrong. A "1-month supply for one person" providing 1,200 calories per day is a different product than one providing 2,000.

## What happened to Wise Company?

Wise Company was founded in Salt Lake City, Utah in 2008 and spent roughly a decade selling emergency food buckets through retail and online channels. The company rebranded under the ReadyWise name around 2019, keeping the same manufacturing process, the same bucket-style packaging, and the same distribution network through retailers including Walmart, Costco, and Sam's Club.

If you encounter an older reference to "Wise Company emergency food" in a blog post or review site, it refers to the same supplier now operating as ReadyWise. Some retailers still index the product under the Wise Company name while the current packaging reads ReadyWise. When comparison-shopping, check the product's serving count and UPC against ReadyWise's current catalog (readywise.com) to confirm you are looking at the same item.

The rebranding was not driven by a product recall or quality issue. It was a repositioning toward a broader prepared-foods market. The production facility and core product formulas continued unchanged.

## What does a ReadyWise emergency food supply include?

A standard ReadyWise emergency bucket is a mix of breakfast pouches and entree pouches. Breakfast options typically include scrambled egg varieties, oatmeal, granola, and pancake mix. Entrees lean on pasta dishes, rice-based meals, and bean dishes. Most kits also include drink mix pouches, commonly an apple-flavored powder or lemonade mix.

A 72-hour ReadyWise kit (approximately 32 servings) gives one adult three full days of meals consumed at the labeled rate. A 1-month bucket typically holds 60 to 120 servings, which covers 30 days at two to three servings per day for a single person. Multi-month supplies come as multiple buckets, often shipped on a pallet.

One detail the marketing sidesteps: every entree requires water for preparation. Pouches are reconstituted with boiling water, typically one to two cups per serving, followed by a 12 to 15-minute wait. If your emergency has cut power or limited your clean water supply, you need a separate heat source and a stored water supply alongside the food. A camp stove and propane canisters handle the heat side. The water side requires separate planning (see the calorie and water section below).

## How many calories do ReadyWise kits actually provide per day?

This calculation matters more than the serving count on the label. The USDA Dietary Guidelines for Americans sets 2,000 calories per day as the general reference intake for adults ([USDA Dietary Guidelines 2020-2025](https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/)). Many ReadyWise kits marketed as a "1-month supply for one person" provide closer to 1,400 to 1,800 calories per day at the standard labeled serving rate, not 2,000.

To find the actual daily intake for any kit, use this formula:

Total calories in kit / Number of days covered = Calories per day

A bucket listing 1,800 total servings at 200 calories each covers 360,000 calories. Spread over 30 days, that is 12,000 calories per day, which sounds excessive. But most 1-month buckets list far fewer total servings, typically 60 to 90, at 200 to 400 calories each. A 90-serving bucket at 300 calories per serving gives 27,000 total calories, divided by 30 days, equals 900 calories per day. That is not a month's supply at full nutrition for an adult.

Run the numbers on whatever kit you are considering before ordering. The calorie count per day is the honest measure of what you are buying.

Short-term calorie restriction is manageable for healthy adults under sedentary emergency conditions. But for children, pregnant women, or anyone exerting physical energy during a response scenario, budget for higher intake. Supplement the base kit with calorie-dense additions: peanut butter pouches, rolled oats, or cooking oil.

## How long does a ReadyWise emergency food supply last on the shelf?

ReadyWise advertises a 25-year shelf life for its freeze-dried pouches under ideal storage conditions: 55 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit, low humidity, and away from direct light. That figure represents the manufacturer's maximum under optimal conditions, not an average across storage environments.

Temperature is the primary variable. Heat accelerates the oxidation reactions that break down fats and degrade nutritional content inside sealed pouches, regardless of the nitrogen flush or oxygen-absorber packets included. A pouch stored at 90 degrees Fahrenheit in a garage will lose quality faster than one stored at 65 degrees in a climate-controlled room, even if both pouches are nominally sealed for 25 years.

Practical storage checklist:
- Climate-controlled interior room, ideally 55 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit year-round
- Off concrete floors (moisture wicks through concrete; use wood shelving or a pallet)
- Away from windows or heat sources
- Stacked no higher than three buckets to avoid crushing the bottom unit's seal
- One pouch inspected per year from a long-term stored bucket as a quality check

For most households storing in a conditioned living space in a temperate climate, a conservative realistic expectancy is 10 to 15 years before quality noticeably declines, even if the bucket claims 25. Garages and attics in hot climates cut that estimate substantially.

## How do ReadyWise kits compare to other emergency food brands?

The main competitors are Mountain House, Augason Farms, and My Patriot Supply (4Patriots). All use freeze-dried or dehydrated food in a similar format. The table below covers the dimensions that actually differ between them.

| Brand | Shelf Life Claim | Packaging | Est. Cal/Day (1-mo kit) | Sodium per Serving |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ReadyWise (Wise Company) | 25 years | Resealable bucket | 1,400 to 1,800 | 600 to 900 mg |
| Mountain House | 30 years | No. 10 cans or pouches | 1,600 to 2,000 | 550 to 850 mg |
| Augason Farms | 25 years | No. 10 cans or pails | 1,200 to 1,800 | 550 to 1,100 mg |
| My Patriot Supply | 25 years | Resealable bucket | 1,500 to 1,800 | 600 to 950 mg |

Sodium is worth tracking for anyone managing cardiovascular health. At two to three servings per day of freeze-dried entrees, daily sodium from food alone hits 1,800 to 2,700 mg. The FDA sets 2,300 mg as the daily limit for healthy adults. People on sodium-restricted diets should read the nutrition panel on individual pouches, not just the bucket label.

Mountain House holds the longest shelf-life claim in the category (30 years) and uses nitrogen-flushed No. 10 cans on some lines, which provide a more reliable seal than resealable bucket lids for long-term storage. Its entrees score better on taste in independent comparisons. ReadyWise has a price and availability advantage: it sits on shelf at major retailers nationwide, which lets you inspect the manufacturing date before committing to a large order.

## What should you check before buying an emergency food supply?

Five checks to run before ordering:

1. **Calories per day, not servings per bucket.** Calculate total kit calories divided by days covered. Below 1,500 calories per day is lean for active adults. Below 1,200 is a concern for any scenario longer than a week.

2. **Serving sizes on the nutrition label, not the front panel.** Some kits list a half-cup dry as a single serving to inflate the serving count. The nutrition label is the legal document. The front-panel serving count is marketing.

3. **Dietary restrictions.** Standard ReadyWise buckets contain gluten, dairy, and soy in most entrees. ReadyWise does offer a separate gluten-free product line, but it is a distinct SKU, not an option within the standard kit. If your household includes anyone with celiac disease or a severe dairy allergy, the standard bucket is not a safe default.

4. **Storage space and temperature.** A year's supply for one adult requires six to eight standard ReadyWise buckets, which takes up roughly 20 to 30 cubic feet of shelf space. Confirm you have a consistently cool location before ordering that volume.

5. **Water requirement alongside the food.** Most ReadyWise entrees require one to two cups of boiling water per serving. A 30-day supply at two servings per day means roughly 15 to 30 gallons of water used for food preparation alone, not drinking. [Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/food) recommends storing a minimum of one gallon of water per person per day for drinking and basic sanitation. For a household of four preparing for 30 days, that is 120 gallons minimum for drinking and sanitation before accounting for cooking water.

Water storage is consistently the step that households skip when they plan around food. Budget the two together.

## FAQ

### Is Wise Company the same as ReadyWise?

Yes. Wise Company rebranded as ReadyWise around 2019. The product line, Salt Lake City operations, and packaging format continued under the new name. If you find a Wise Company product at a discount, check the expiration or best-by date on the individual pouch, not just the outer bucket label, before purchasing.

### How many people does a ReadyWise 1-month supply feed?

The standard ReadyWise 1-month bucket is sized for one adult. At 60 to 120 servings consumed at two to three servings per day, it covers 30 days for one person. To feed a family of four for a month, you need four of those buckets, or a larger multi-person kit sold as a specific SKU. Confirm the per-person sizing before ordering based on the family unit, not just the "1-month" label.

### Do ReadyWise kits require cooking equipment?

Most ReadyWise entrees require boiling water and 12 to 15 minutes of rehydration time in the sealed pouch. You need a heat source and clean water. A camp stove with propane canisters covers both requirements. Without a heat source, options narrow to the cold-soak-friendly pouches (some granolas and drink mixes) and any non-rehydrated items in the kit. A propane burner or alcohol stove is a practical addition to any food storage setup.

### What is the best storage method for an emergency food bucket?

Store buckets in a climate-controlled room between 55 and 70 degrees Fahrenheit, away from windows and moisture sources. Keep them off concrete floors on a wooden shelf or pallet. Stack no higher than three units. The resealable lid is designed for daily use after opening; for long-term storage of an unopened bucket, the original factory seal is more reliable. Inspect one pouch annually to check for off-odors or color changes.

### Can you buy ReadyWise at Costco or Walmart in-store?

ReadyWise kits are stocked at Costco, Walmart, and Sam's Club in many regions, as well as at REI and Bass Pro Shops. Availability varies by location and season. Buying in-store gives you the chance to check the manufacturing date on the bucket before committing to a large order, which is worth doing if you plan to hold the supply for several years.
