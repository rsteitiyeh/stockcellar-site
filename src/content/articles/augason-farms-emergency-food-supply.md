An Augason Farms emergency food supply is a packaged set of freeze-dried and dehydrated meals sold in #10 cans, resealable pails, or pre-built kit bundles. The most commonly purchased format is a 30-day, 1-person pail containing roughly 30,000 to 55,000 total calories depending on the specific product line. That range matters: a pail providing 1,000 calories per day is a very different purchase than one providing 1,800, and both can legally carry a "30-day" label if the fine print defines a serving as a 150-calorie portion.

The rest of this guide covers what the kits actually contain, how to run the calorie math before buying, what you need alongside the food bucket to make it functional in a real emergency, and how shelf life claims hold up against realistic storage conditions.

## What does an Augason Farms emergency food supply kit include?

A standard Augason Farms emergency pail contains a mix of freeze-dried and dehydrated items oriented toward familiar American cooking: oatmeal and grain cereals for breakfast, pasta and rice-based dishes for entrees, and powdered drink mixes including fruit-flavored beverages. Common item names include creamy chicken-flavored rice, cheesy broccoli and rice, potato soup, and sweetened multigrain cereals.

The pail format uses sealed Mylar-lined pouches inside a stackable plastic bucket. Augason Farms also sells individual #10 cans for specific ingredient categories: hard red wheat, white rice, rolled oats, powdered milk, and freeze-dried vegetables and fruits. Those cans are sold individually and as components in larger bundled systems intended for multi-month household reserves.

One structural note: most Augason Farms entrees are built around a starch base (pasta, rice, potato) with flavoring powder and a small amount of dehydrated protein or vegetable. Protein per serving typically runs 3 to 7 grams. If household members are doing physical labor during an emergency, that protein floor will not hold. A separate protein source, whether canned fish, peanut butter, or powdered egg, is a practical addition to any setup relying on these kits as the primary supply.

## How many calories does a 30-day Augason Farms pail actually deliver?

The honest range is 1,200 to 1,833 calories per day, depending on which specific product line you buy. The USDA Dietary Guidelines for Americans uses 2,000 calories per day as the reference intake for a moderately active adult ([USDA Dietary Guidelines 2020-2025](https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/)). Most 30-day single-person pails fall below that benchmark.

Calculate it yourself before ordering. Find the total calorie count on the product listing, divide by 30, and compare the result to your household's actual daily need. Alternatively, use the serving data from the nutrition label:

Total servings x Calories per serving / 30 days = Calories per day

A pail listing 307 servings at 180 calories each delivers 55,260 total calories, which divided by 30 gives 1,842 calories per day. For a sedentary adult during a short disruption, that is serviceable. For a 190-pound adult moving supplies, managing children, or working outdoors in cold weather, 1,842 calories per day creates a real deficit within a few days.

The practical planning adjustment: take the labeled day count, multiply by 0.85, and treat that as your realistic coverage for a physically active adult. A 30-day pail at 1,800 calories per day covers roughly 25 to 27 days at normal adult activity levels.

## What is the realistic shelf life of Augason Farms food?

Augason Farms claims up to 25 years for freeze-dried products and up to 30 years for individual ingredient items like white rice and hard red wheat, both under optimal conditions. Those conditions are specific: consistent temperature of 55 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit, low humidity, no direct light, and undisturbed sealed packaging.

Temperature is the dominant variable. Heat drives oxidation reactions that break down fats and degrade vitamins inside sealed pouches regardless of nitrogen flushing or oxygen-absorber packets. A pouch stored at 90 degrees Fahrenheit in a garage degrades several times faster than one stored at 65 degrees in a climate-controlled room, even if both pouches are nominally sealed. In a hot climate, a realistic working estimate for mixed pails stored in a non-conditioned space is 8 to 12 years, not 25.

There is also an important distinction between freeze-dried and dehydrated products within the same pail. Freeze-dried items retain more nutrients and texture and tend to hit the longer shelf life claims. Dehydrated items are less expensive but typically carry 10 to 15 year ratings under the same storage conditions. Most mixed Augason Farms pails contain both. The pail label reflects the longest-lasting item in it. Check individual pouch labels for the specific rating on each component, especially if you plan to hold the supply for more than ten years.

Once a pouch is opened, reseal it and use the contents within a week. The long shelf life claim applies only to unopened packaging.

## What do you need alongside an emergency food bucket?

Water is the first and largest gap. Every Augason Farms entree is a dry product that requires water to prepare, typically 1.5 to 2 cups of boiling water per serving, followed by a 10 to 15 minute rehydration wait. At three meals per day using 1.5 cups of water per meal, one person uses 4.5 cups of water per day on cooking alone, separate from drinking and sanitation.

FEMA's Ready.gov recommends a minimum of one gallon per person per day for drinking and basic sanitation ([Ready.gov food and water in an emergency](https://www.ready.gov/food)). When you add cooking water, the practical daily water budget per adult using an Augason Farms kit runs closer to 1.5 gallons. A 30-day reserve for one adult requires 40 to 45 gallons of stored water when you count both uses.

A few additional items are needed before the food bucket is functional:

- A reliable heat source: camp stove with propane, butane burner, or wood-burning rocket stove
- Fuel sufficient for 90 cooking sessions over 30 days (three meals per day)
- A medium pot, eating utensils, and a cutting board
- A backup water purification method such as a ceramic filter or purification tablets

The heat source requirement is easy to underestimate. In the scenarios that make emergency food most relevant, power is unavailable. A two-burner propane camp stove and a case of one-pound propane canisters runs roughly $40 to $60 and handles 30 days of boiling water with capacity to spare. Pair the stove with the food bucket before calling the supply complete.

## How does the cost per calorie break down for Augason Farms?

Standard retail pricing for a 30-day, 1-person Augason Farms pail runs roughly $80 to $150 depending on the specific kit and retailer. At 55,000 total calories for a well-stocked pail, that works out to approximately $1.45 to $2.73 per 1,000 calories.

Comparison points for context: bulk white rice from a warehouse store runs $0.20 to $0.35 per 1,000 calories, and bulk rolled oats are similar. Canned beans average $0.50 to $1.00 per 1,000 calories. The premium on an Augason Farms pail covers convenience, guaranteed shelf life without active rotation, and portion structure that removes daily calculation. It also covers the freeze-drying and nitrogen-flushing infrastructure that a household cannot reproduce at home.

For budget-conscious planning, a hybrid approach delivers better value at equivalent caloric coverage. One Augason Farms pail handles the first 25 to 27 days when stress is high and time is short. Bulk staples, rice, oats, lentils, and canned proteins, cover the weeks beyond that at a fraction of the per-calorie cost. A 50-pound bag of white rice costs around $20 and provides roughly 80,000 calories. Pairing a single pail with 50 pounds of rice and a case of canned tuna gives a household more total calories at lower cost than buying a second pail.

## FAQ

### How much water does an Augason Farms emergency food supply require?

Plan for 1.5 to 2 cups of water per serving for cooking alone. At three meals per day, that is 4.5 to 6 cups of cooking water per adult daily. FEMA recommends at least 1 gallon per person per day for drinking and sanitation. Add cooking water on top of that, and the practical daily budget is closer to 1.5 gallons per adult. A 30-day single-person supply needs 40 to 45 gallons of total stored water to be functional.

### Does Augason Farms emergency food require cooking skills?

No. Most entrees are reconstituted by adding boiling water to the pouch, stirring, resealing, and waiting 10 to 15 minutes. The main practical requirement is a reliable heat source and clean water, not cooking experience. A propane camp stove is the standard pairing. Without a heat source, options shrink to dry-mix items like granola, flavored drink powders, and any cold-soak-compatible grain products in the kit.

### Are Augason Farms products gluten-free?

Standard Augason Farms pails are not gluten-free. They contain pasta, wheat-based cereals, and flavoring components that include wheat derivatives. Individual ingredient cans of white rice, freeze-dried fruits, and freeze-dried vegetables are naturally gluten-free, but mixed entree kits are not safe for anyone with celiac disease. Augason Farms offers a dedicated gluten-free product line as a separate SKU. Verify the allergen statement on the specific product before ordering for a household with gluten sensitivity.

### What is the difference between Augason Farms freeze-dried and dehydrated products?

Freeze-dried products are processed at very low temperatures under a vacuum, which removes moisture while leaving cellular structure largely intact. They reconstitute faster, retain more nutrients, taste closer to the original food, and carry the longer shelf life claims (up to 25 years). Dehydrated products are processed with heat, which is less expensive but degrades some vitamins and changes texture. They typically carry 10 to 15 year shelf life ratings. Many Augason Farms pails contain both types. The individual pouch label specifies which process was used.

### Can you buy Augason Farms emergency food at Costco or Walmart?

Augason Farms products are stocked at Walmart, Sam's Club, and through the company's own website. Costco availability varies by region and often follows seasonal or event-driven restocking cycles. Buying in-store lets you check the manufacturing date printed on the bucket before committing to a large purchase, which is worth doing for any supply you plan to hold for several years. The manufacturing date is separate from the best-by date and gives you a more accurate picture of how much usable shelf life remains.
