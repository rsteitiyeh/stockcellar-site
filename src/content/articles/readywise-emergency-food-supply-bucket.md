A ReadyWise emergency food supply bucket is a resealable plastic pail packed with individually sealed foil pouches of freeze-dried and dehydrated meals. Most standard buckets are sized for one adult and contain 60 to 120 servings. The critical pre-purchase check is daily calorie total: many "1-month" kits deliver 900 to 1,400 calories per adult per day, well short of the 2,000-calorie adult reference intake.

That gap between label claim and actual daily calories is where most emergency food purchases go wrong. The serving count printed on the bucket exterior is a marketing number. The useful number is total kit calories divided by the number of days you need the supply to cover.

## What is in a ReadyWise emergency food supply bucket?

A standard ReadyWise bucket is a 3.5 to 6-gallon stackable plastic pail with a resealable lid. Inside are individual foil pouches, each sealed with nitrogen and oxygen absorbers. Contents typically include scrambled egg varieties, oatmeal, granola, pasta dishes, mac and cheese, and rice-based entrees. Most kits add a few pouches of drink mix, usually apple cider or lemonade powder.

One point worth knowing before you crack open a bucket: the resealable lid is designed for daily access after first opening, not for long-term storage protection. The individual foil pouches are the actual preservation unit. The nitrogen-flushed, oxygen-free environment inside each pouch produces the claimed 25-year shelf life. The bucket holds the pouches, keeps them organized, and makes the kit portable. It does not extend shelf life on its own. If a pouch develops a pinhole, the lid provides no backup. If a pouch remains intact, moving it to a different container has no effect on its shelf life at all.

## How many calories does a ReadyWise bucket actually provide per day?

Most standard ReadyWise buckets provide 900 to 1,600 calories per adult per day at the labeled serving rate, not the 2,000-calorie reference intake established by the USDA Dietary Guidelines for Americans ([USDA Dietary Guidelines 2020-2025](https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/)).

The serving count inflates the perceived supply. ReadyWise lists some servings as a half-cup dry measure or one cup prepared, at 150 to 250 calories per serving. Reaching 2,000 calories requires six to ten servings per day. A bucket marketed as a "1-month supply for one person" containing 120 total servings covers only 20 days at six servings per day, not 30.

The formula to run before ordering any bucket:

**Total calories in kit / Days of coverage needed = Calories per day**

A 90-serving bucket averaging 250 calories per serving contains 22,500 total calories. Divided over 30 days: 750 calories per adult per day. That is significant caloric restriction, not a month's supply by any standard nutritional measure.

Short-term caloric restriction is manageable for healthy adults in sedentary conditions. For children, physically active scenarios, or anyone with elevated medical needs, budget higher. Supplement any bucket purchase with calorie-dense shelf-stable additions: peanut butter, rolled oats, cooking oil, and nuts add calories cheaply and store well alongside the bucket.

## How long do ReadyWise emergency food buckets last on the shelf?

ReadyWise claims a 25-year shelf life for its freeze-dried pouches under ideal conditions: 55 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit, low humidity, and no direct light. That figure is for the pouches themselves, not the assembled bucket.

**Temperature** is the primary shelf-life variable. Heat accelerates oxidation of the fats inside each pouch, even inside an oxygen-free environment. Pouches stored at 85 degrees Fahrenheit in an unconditioned garage degrade measurably faster than pouches held at 65 degrees in an interior room, regardless of the nitrogen flush inside.

**Seal integrity** matters separately from temperature. A pouch in good condition feels slightly firm when squeezed gently. A flat or deflated pouch may have admitted oxygen through a compromised seam. Inspect one pouch per year from each long-term bucket to catch failures early rather than at the moment you need the food.

**Food type** within the bucket affects the timeline as well. ReadyWise uses both freeze-dried and dehydrated ingredients across its product line. Freeze-dried entrees retain quality longer. Dehydrated fruit and vegetable components may lose texture and flavor faster than the entrees in the same bucket.

A conservative estimate for a bucket stored in a conditioned living space in a temperate climate is 10 to 15 years of good quality. The 25-year maximum requires consistent near-ideal conditions throughout the full storage period.

## How should you store a ReadyWise emergency food bucket?

FEMA recommends storing emergency food in a cool, dry location and checking expiration dates on a regular schedule ([FEMA Ready.gov food storage guidance](https://www.ready.gov/food)).

For ReadyWise buckets specifically:

- Temperature between 55 and 70 degrees Fahrenheit year-round. An interior closet or conditioned basement outperforms a garage. Garages in warm climates can reach 90 degrees in summer, cutting realistic shelf life to under 10 years.
- Off concrete floors. Moisture migrates through concrete over months and can affect the outer bucket. A wooden shelf or plastic pallet keeps buckets elevated and dry.
- Stack no more than three units high. Four or more stacked buckets compress the base unit; the lid and base can deform under sustained weight, which may affect the reseal function after opening.
- Away from windows and heat sources. UV light degrades foil pouch material over years, even without direct sun.
- Track best-by dates on individual pouches, not just the bucket exterior. ReadyWise prints best-by dates on each foil pouch. Batches within a single bucket can vary by manufacturing date.
- Cook one pouch per variety per year. This confirms palatability, catches any seal failures, and lets your household practice preparation on a camp stove before an actual emergency.

## What should you check before buying an emergency food supply bucket?

**1. Daily calories, calculated.** Divide total kit calories by days needed. Under 1,500 per day is lean for most adults under sedentary conditions. Under 1,200 per day for scenarios longer than a week is a concern. Supplement the base bucket if the math falls short.

**2. Water requirement.** ReadyWise entrees require one to two cups of boiling water per serving to rehydrate. At six servings per adult per day, add half a gallon of cooking water per person on top of drinking and sanitation water. FEMA's minimum for drinking and sanitation is one gallon per person per day. A two-week supply for one adult requires roughly 21 gallons of water for cooking alone.

**3. Heat source.** Every ReadyWise entree requires hot water to prepare properly. Without a camp stove, propane burner, or equivalent, usable options narrow to granola, drink mixes, and a few cold-soak-compatible pouches. Those cover a fraction of the bucket's calories. Budget a heat source before ordering the food.

**4. Allergens.** Standard ReadyWise buckets contain gluten, milk, and soy in most entrees. ReadyWise publishes allergen information by SKU. For households with celiac disease, severe dairy allergies, or other restrictions, verify individual pouch ingredient lists, not just the bucket summary label.

**5. Storage space.** A 1-month supply for one adult is one to two standard ReadyWise buckets. A year's supply runs six to eight buckets, roughly 25 to 35 cubic feet of shelf space. Measure the storage location before committing to a large order.

## FAQ

**How much water does a ReadyWise emergency food bucket require?**

Plan half a gallon of cooking water per adult per day at six servings per day. Add that to FEMA's minimum of one gallon per person per day for drinking and sanitation. For two adults covering two weeks, that totals roughly 42 gallons for drinking and sanitation, plus 14 gallons for cooking. Water planning is the step households skip most often when organizing around food buckets.

**Can a ReadyWise bucket feed more than one person?**

A bucket labeled "1-month supply for 1 person" feeds two people for half the labeled duration and four people for a quarter. Serving count stays fixed; coverage shrinks with headcount. For multi-person households, multiply bucket count by the number of adults and adjust downward for children, who typically need 1,200 to 1,600 calories per day rather than 2,000. Always verify total kit calories before scaling up.

**What is the difference between ReadyWise Emergency and Adventure bucket lines?**

ReadyWise markets distinct product lines for different uses. The Emergency Supply line targets cost-per-calorie efficiency with simpler meal options. The Adventure line uses higher-calorie entrees and broader variety. Calorie density per serving, price per calorie, and meal variety differ between the lines. Compare total kit calories and price per calorie across specific SKUs rather than relying on the line name alone.

**Does opening the bucket reduce the remaining food's shelf life?**

Minimally, as long as the individual pouches stay sealed. The bucket lid is not the preservation unit; the foil pouches are. A reclosed bucket with all pouches still factory-sealed can be stored again with no significant effect on shelf life. Once a pouch is opened, eat the contents within that meal or the next day. Do not reseal an open pouch and treat it as long-term storage.
