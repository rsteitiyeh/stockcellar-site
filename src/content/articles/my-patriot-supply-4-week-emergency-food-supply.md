A My Patriot Supply 4-week emergency food supply is a set of freeze-dried and dehydrated meal pouches packed into resealable buckets, sized to feed one adult for 28 days. Most kits in this category provide 1,500 to 2,000 calories per day per person at the labeled serving rate. Whether that number holds under your household's actual conditions depends on how you read the nutrition label, how much physical activity the emergency involves, and whether you have water and a heat source in place.

That 28-day window is the first thing to confirm. A "4-week" kit covers 28 days, not 30. If you are planning against a specific duration, the two-day gap matters when rationing is tight and you assumed a full month.

## What does a My Patriot Supply 4-week emergency food supply include?

A standard My Patriot Supply 4-week kit contains breakfast, lunch, and dinner pouches sized for one adult. Breakfast options typically include oatmeal, granola, and freeze-dried scrambled egg varieties. Lunch and dinner pouches lean on rice-based dishes, pasta entrees, bean soups, and potato-based meals. Many kits include drink mix packets as well, usually fruit-flavored powders.

The pouches are sealed with a nitrogen flush to displace oxygen, then packed into a resealable plastic bucket. The resealable lid functions well for repeated daily use once you open the kit. For long-term uninterrupted storage, the original factory seal on the bucket is the more reliable barrier. Once you break that seal, treat the bucket as active inventory and consume pouches within the storage guidelines printed on each individual pouch.

Protein content is a consistent gap across this product category. Most entrees deliver 4 to 8 grams of protein per serving, built around a starch base with flavoring powder and a small measure of dehydrated protein or vegetable. If household members will be doing physical work during an emergency, that floor will not hold without supplementation. Adding canned fish, peanut butter pouches, or powdered whole eggs to the kit addresses the protein deficit at low cost without requiring refrigeration.

## How many calories does the kit actually provide per day?

My Patriot Supply advertises approximately 2,000 calories per day on its flagship 4-week kit for one adult, which aligns with the USDA Dietary Guidelines for Americans reference intake of 2,000 calories per day for a moderately active adult ([USDA Dietary Guidelines 2020-2025](https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/)). That is among the stronger calorie claims in the category. Many competing 4-week kits land closer to 1,400 to 1,600 calories per day at the labeled serving rate.

Verify it yourself before ordering. The total calorie count and serving data are listed on the product detail page. The formula is straightforward:

Total servings x Calories per serving / 28 days = Calories per day

A kit listing 280 servings at 200 calories each delivers 56,000 total calories divided by 28 days, giving 2,000 calories per day. A kit listing 140 servings at 250 calories each delivers 35,000 total calories divided by 28, giving 1,250 calories per day. Same label, very different purchase. The serving count on the front of the bucket is marketing until you cross-check it against the nutrition facts panel.

Sedentary adults in a short disruption manage adequately on lower intakes. Children, pregnant women, and adults performing physical labor need more. Plan for the realistic scenario in your household, not the best-case scenario on the product page.

## What storage conditions does a My Patriot Supply kit require?

My Patriot Supply claims 25 years of shelf life for its freeze-dried products under optimal conditions: 55 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit, low humidity, kept away from direct light, in undisturbed sealed packaging.

Temperature is the primary variable. Heat drives oxidation reactions that break down fats and degrade vitamins inside sealed pouches regardless of nitrogen flushing or oxygen-absorber packets. A pouch stored at 90 degrees Fahrenheit in an uninsulated garage degrades substantially faster than one kept at 65 degrees in a climate-controlled interior room, even if both are technically sealed and nominally rated for 25 years. In warm-climate states, a garage or attic is not a viable long-term storage location. A practical working estimate for mixed pouches without climate control in a hot climate is 8 to 12 years before quality noticeably declines.

Concrete floors pull moisture through them. Store buckets on wooden shelving or a pallet. Stack no more than three buckets per column to avoid deforming the bottom unit's lid seal under sustained load. Once a year, open one pouch from a long-term stored bucket and check for off odors, color changes, or moisture inside the inner bag. A failed pouch detected early lets you rotate stock rather than discovering the problem when you need the supply.

## What do you need alongside a 4-week emergency food supply?

Water is the largest gap, and it is the item households most often skip when planning around a food bucket. Every My Patriot Supply pouch is a dry product that requires boiling water to prepare, typically 1.5 to 2 cups per serving, followed by a 10 to 15 minute rehydration period inside the sealed pouch.

FEMA's Ready.gov recommends storing at least one gallon per person per day for drinking and basic sanitation ([Ready.gov: Food and Water in an Emergency](https://www.ready.gov/food)). When cooking water is included at two to three meals per day, the practical daily water budget per adult using a freeze-dried kit runs closer to 1.5 gallons. A 28-day single-person supply needs 40 to 45 gallons of total stored water to be functional, separate from any water used for washing.

A heat source is also a hard requirement. Without power, freeze-dried pouches are not usable without an alternative. A two-burner propane camp stove with a case of one-pound propane canisters handles 28 days of boiling water for one person and costs roughly $40 to $60 at outdoor retailers. A backup water purification method, either a ceramic gravity filter or iodine tablets, covers the scenario where stored water runs low.

Practical checklist for a complete 4-week setup per adult in the household:

- One My Patriot Supply 4-week bucket per adult
- 40 to 45 gallons of stored water (drinking, sanitation, cooking)
- A camp stove with propane or butane and fuel for approximately 85 boiling sessions
- A backup water purification method
- A medium pot, utensils, and camp bowl
- Supplemental protein, canned fish, peanut butter, or powdered eggs

Ordering the food bucket first and filling in the rest later is how most households end up with incomplete kits. The bucket alone is not a functional emergency supply without water and a heat source.

## What should you verify before buying a 4-week emergency food supply?

**Calories per day, not serving count.** Divide total kit calories by 28. Below 1,500 calories per day is lean for a healthy sedentary adult in a low-exertion scenario. Below 1,200 calories is a concern for any situation lasting more than a few days.

**Serving size on the nutrition label, not the front panel.** Some kits define a serving as a half-cup dry measure to inflate the serving count. The nutrition facts label is the legal reference; the front-panel serving count is marketing until confirmed.

**Dietary restrictions.** Standard My Patriot Supply kits contain gluten, soy, and dairy components in most entrees. If anyone in the household has celiac disease or a severe dairy allergy, the standard kit is not a safe default. Check the allergen statement on the specific kit before ordering.

**Household size.** A "4-week supply for 1 person" divided equally among four adults covers roughly seven days. Size the order to the number of adults in the household, not the labeled duration.

**Sodium per day.** Most freeze-dried entrees run 600 to 950 mg of sodium per serving. At two to three servings per day, daily sodium from the kit alone approaches the FDA's recommended daily limit of 2,300 mg for healthy adults. Anyone managing blood pressure or cardiovascular health should read the per-serving sodium figure on each pouch before committing to a 4-week supply of any brand.

## FAQ

### How many servings are in a My Patriot Supply 4-week emergency food supply?

Most My Patriot Supply 4-week kits for one adult contain roughly 280 to 310 servings across breakfast, lunch, and dinner pouches. The specific count varies by product line and formulation year. The figure to verify is total kit calories, not serving count, since individual pouch calorie densities differ. Divide total calories by 28 to confirm the daily average matches your household's actual needs.

### Is the My Patriot Supply 4-week kit large enough for more than one person?

No. A "4-week supply for 1 person" sized for one adult does not scale across a household without buying additional kits. At two adults, the kit covers roughly 14 days. At four adults, closer to 7 days. My Patriot Supply sells multi-person kits sized for specific household counts, or you can order multiples of the single-person kit. Match the number of kits to the number of people.

### Does a My Patriot Supply 4-week kit require cooking skills?

No. Most entrees are prepared by adding boiling water to the pouch, stirring, resealing it, and waiting 10 to 15 minutes. The practical requirements are a heat source and clean water, not cooking experience. Without a heat source, usable items narrow to cold-soak-compatible pouches like granola or drink mixes. Plan a camp stove alongside the kit from the start.

### Can you use My Patriot Supply meals as part of everyday meal rotation?

Yes. Freeze-dried and dehydrated pouches work as pantry staples and can rotate into regular meals as the supply ages. The cost per meal is higher than bulk staples, and sodium content is high for daily long-term use. The practical approach is first-in, first-out rotation: consume the oldest pouches in regular cooking and replace them, which keeps the supply fresh and avoids waste.

### What is the difference between My Patriot Supply and 4Patriots?

My Patriot Supply is the company; 4Patriots is a brand operating under the same parent organization. Both sell overlapping emergency food products. If you encounter both names while researching, you are looking at products from the same corporate entity. Verify the specific product's nutrition facts, allergen statement, and calorie total regardless of which brand name appears on the bucket.
